# Sound design and audio post-production standard

Mandatory for American Wallet and Ninety Sense. Claude is the sound designer, music editor, effects editor, and final mix supervisor. Sound is designed with the picture, not added as an afterthought. The goal is professional, intentional, emotionally precise sound—not maximum noise. Every sound must have a story purpose, correct timing, believable perspective, and a controlled place in the mix.

## 1. Story-first sound direction
1. Read the complete script and understand its subject, emotional progression, pace, and payoff.
2. Listen to the complete voiceover and mark actual timing, pauses, emphasis, breaths, and transitions.
3. Build the sound map alongside the visual beat map.
4. Decide where sound should create realism, tension, curiosity, intimacy, surprise, relief, contrast, or silence.
5. Design a distinct sonic treatment for this video. Do not reuse one generic music bed and effect pattern on every upload.

Do not force every layer into every scene. A quiet close-up may need only room tone and voice. A reveal may need a musical change, a precise accent, and a short pause. The mix must breathe.

## 2. Required layers
### Voiceover
- Preserve the original performance, meaning, and emotional nuance. Do not cut important words or stretch unnaturally.
- Remove only distracting noise, clicks, harsh breaths, and pauses when appropriate; do not sterilize natural emotion.
- Apply corrective EQ, compression, de-essing, and automation only when needed. Avoid pumping, harsh sibilance, artifacts, or crushed dynamics.
- Keep speech intelligible and forward. Music, ambience, and effects yield to it.
- Check for clicks, clipped syllables, abrupt cuts, and dead air at the beginning and ending.

### Background music
- Select music for the actual story and channel identity, not merely its genre.
- Plan a musical arc: opening/hook, development, escalation or contrast, turning point, and payoff/ending as appropriate.
- Create purposeful changes: add/remove layers, shift intensity, build/release tension, change cue, use a brief dropout, or move between complementary cues.
- Do not leave one unchanged track at the same level from beginning to end.
- Do not change music on every scene cut; follow story sections and musical phrasing.
- Avoid lyrics or prominent melodic hooks that compete with narration unless deliberately justified.
- Use only user-supplied audio or audio available in the authorized project environment. Do not assume a paid library/API or claim a track is licensed unless its license is known.

### Ambience and room tone
- Build believable spaces: home, kitchen, office, street, store, public building, hallway, bedroom, exterior, or stylized world as required.
- Maintain continuity across adjacent shots in the same location; crossfade when moving between spaces.
- Keep ambience subtle and frequency-aware so it does not cloud speech or music.
- Use designed atmosphere for stylized scenes rather than pretending they are live action.

### Foley and story-specific SFX
- Design effects for visible actions and meaningful details: paper, envelopes, page turns, objects set down, footsteps, clothing, doors, phones, typing, switches, impacts, weather, and other actions actually shown.
- Match material, size, distance, surface, and force. A tiny tap must not sound like an explosion.
- Layer foley only when it improves realism. Vary perspective and intensity; not every action needs a sound.
- Never use random stock SFX, repeated notification pings, or generic whooshes to fill gaps.

### Transitions and emphasis
- Design audio transitions with visual transitions: hard cuts, L-cuts, J-cuts, crossfades, sound bridges, reverse swells, risers, impacts, tonal shifts, and brief dropouts where motivated.
- Use a sound bridge for continuity; use a clean cut or silence when separation is stronger.
- Place accents on meaningful reveals, visual changes, key phrases, or action—not automatically on every edit.
- Do not stack impacts, risers, and whooshes on one event. One precise accent is often stronger.
- Never obscure narration or create unwanted loudness jumps.

## 3. Mandatory beat-by-beat sound map
For every meaningful beat specify:
- Voiceover phrase and actual start/end time.
- Music cue/section, intensity, and changes/dropouts.
- Ambience/room tone and continuity from prior scene.
- Foley/SFX event, source/action, exact sync point, and perspective.
- Transition treatment into and out of the beat.
- Relative level/priority and ducking/automation.
- Intended emotional or informational effect.

Use actual voiceover timing when available. If timing cannot be inspected, label it estimated. Make entries specific: "soft paper shuffle as the envelope enters frame, followed by a restrained paper tear on the opening action" is useful; "add cinematic SFX" is not.

## 4. Mixing hierarchy and delivery
Priority: (1) intelligible, natural voiceover; (2) story-critical action/information effects; (3) supportive music; (4) ambience and texture.

- Automate music and ambience down under speech; do not rely only on a static low volume.
- Let selected SFX briefly come forward in narration gaps, then return them to the soundscape.
- Use EQ/space to prevent layers masking speech.
- Control low-frequency buildup, harsh highs, clipping, distortion, and sudden level jumps.
- Use smooth fades/crossfades; preserve dynamics rather than crushing every scene.
- Check headphones and ordinary speakers if available; mono-check when possible.
- Keep stereo placement stable and intentional. Never pan essential dialogue out of reach.
- Use a sensible web-video loudness target appropriate to source/platform (commonly around -14 to -16 LUFS integrated for a typical YouTube mix) and keep true peaks safely below clipping (commonly no higher than -1 dBTP). These are targets, not reasons to damage dynamics. If tools cannot measure loudness/true peak, do not claim measurement.

## 5. Channel-specific sonic identity
### American Wallet
Documentary, credible, restrained, human, and financially grounded. Use subtle tension, investigative curiosity, measured momentum, empathy, and thoughtful payoff. Prefer naturalistic location sound and tactile foley: paper, mail, receipts, household activity, office/bank/store ambience, footsteps, and real objects when shown. Avoid sensational trailer booms, melodramatic horror drones, cartoon money sounds, casino clichés, and constant ticking clocks. Music should support trust and attention, not sound like an advertisement or political attack.

### Ninety Sense
Emotionally expressive, psychologically precise, and cinematic within its stylized animated world. Use designed sonic details for actions and emotional cues: phone vibration, message sounds, awkward room tone, footsteps, breath, silence, object movement, subtle tension textures, and intentional comedic/unsettling accents. Music can move from unease or curiosity to tension, realization, sadness, relief, or dark humor according to the story. Avoid generic motivational music, random meme effects, and constant dramatic impacts.

## 6. Source and asset discipline
- First use audio supplied by the user or available in the current authorized project environment.
- If a required sound cannot be found, identify the exact missing cue and request the asset or permission to use a specific source. Do not invent an audio file, pretend a sound was generated, or add an unlicensed track.
- Do not require a new paid API/subscription.
- Keep original files intact. Organize audio by type and scene/cue; retain source/license notes when available.
- Do not download from unauthorized sources or assume online audio is free to use.

## 7. Full audio quality-control pass
Review the complete video from beginning to end with picture and sound together. Check:
- Voiceover is clear, natural, present, and intelligible throughout.
- No words are masked, clipped, cut off, or covered by effects.
- Music has a deliberate arc and is not an unchanged generic loop.
- Music changes, drops, builds, and ending are motivated by the story.
- Ambience matches locations and transitions smoothly.
- Foley/SFX match visible actions, material, timing, and perspective.
- Transition sounds are selective and synchronized, not repetitive or excessive.
- No accidental silence, missing audio, abrupt cue ends, clicks, pops, distortion, clipping, or startling level jumps.
- No competing bass buildup or harsh frequency clashes.
- Ending resolves cleanly with an intentional audio tail.
- The mix feels like one coherent soundtrack, not a pile of effects.

Fix issues and replay affected sections in context. Recheck the opening and ending after revisions.

## 8. Capability honesty
Use only audio tools, files, and editing operations genuinely available in the current environment. Claude can design the soundscape and produce a detailed sound map without access to an audio library. If it cannot source, place, mix, or measure a sound, state exactly what is missing and request the asset/access. Never claim a finished professional mix, measured loudness, licensed music, or completed audio QC unless those operations actually happened.
