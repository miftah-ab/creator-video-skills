# Ninety Sense — channel profile

## Per-project frame format

Ninety Sense supports **both 16:9 landscape and 9:16 portrait**. The user selects one format for each production; do not assume Ninety Sense is permanently vertical.

- For **9:16**, stage characters, props, expressions, and action for a vertical phone screen. Keep the main action and captions inside portrait-safe areas.
- For **16:9**, redesign the scene for widescreen. Use horizontal staging, purposeful negative space, foreground/midground/background depth, and clear left-to-right character relationships where useful. Do not stretch a portrait composition or simply place a narrow vertical scene in the middle of a wide frame.
- Adapt camera framing, scene layout, captions, subtitles, and important props to the selected format from the beginning.
- Preserve Ninety Sense's original stylized 3D environment and integrated flat 2D expressive-face identity in either format.

## Audience and content
Psychological and emotional Shorts about overthinking, relationships, identity, loneliness, behavior, and uncomfortable truths. The content must feel like an animated story, not a motivational quote slideshow.

## Visual identity
Use the mixed-media system in `ricks-clips-visual-system.md`:
- Stylized 3D environments, props, lighting, and character bodies.
- Distinctive flat 2D expressive face/head graphics integrated onto characters.
- Physical actions that make invisible psychological states visible.
- Consistent original characters across shots.
- Clear compositions adapted to the selected aspect ratio.

This is a visual-format reference only. Do not copy Ricks Clips scripts, characters, exact designs, footage, voice, music, or branding.

## Story structure
Do not force every psychology script into a prop-based cause-and-effect plot. Instead:
1. Show a recognizable behavior or emotional consequence immediately.
2. Let viewers understand the situation before explaining it.
3. Escalate through visible choices, reactions, and consequences.
4. Reveal the psychological insight through an action or visual reversal.
5. End on a memorable emotional payoff.

Avoid generic advice, random cinematic b-roll, floating quote text, and unrelated luxury imagery.

## Characters
- Establish a small reusable original cast.
- Keep body proportions, outfits, identifying colors, and face treatment consistent.
- Use readable expressions: confusion, suspicion, anxiety, embarrassment, anger, relief, realization.
- Keep the face treatment graphic and intentionally integrated with the 3D scene; do not drift into generic photorealistic faces. Adapt face scale and placement to the selected aspect ratio.
- Preserve character identity, clothing, props, and spatial continuity.
- Never recreate identifiable Ricks Clips characters.

## Typography and color system — mandatory

Ninety Sense uses a premium, dark editorial-psychology identity. Typography must feel cinematic and emotionally precise, never like a generic motivational Shorts template.

### Locked color palette
- **Deep Ink** — `#111827`: primary dark field, title cards, and deep shadows.
- **Slate** — `#374151`: secondary dark surfaces and graphic structure.
- **Warm Ivory** — `#F7F4EE`: primary headline and subtitle color.
- **Muted Vermilion** — `#C94B3C`: rare emphasis for a consequential word, warning, emotional rupture, or key object. Never use as a constant accent.
- **Soft Sand** — `#E8DED0`: secondary text and subtle warm supporting details.
- **White** — `#FFFFFF`: only when maximum contrast is needed.

Use Deep Ink and Slate as the foundation, Warm Ivory for primary type, and Vermilion only as a deliberate accent. Keep 3D materials and lighting believable; do not force every scene into a flat palette. No purple, neon blue/cyan, gradients, glow, rainbow colors, or excessive red.

### Locked font pairing
- **Display / hooks / chapter words: Oswald Bold (700).** Short, high-impact phrases only.
- **Subtitles / captions / labels / supporting copy: Manrope.** Regular (400), Medium (500–600), and Bold (700) for selective emphasis.
- Use only these two families in normal video graphics. No decorative/script fonts or random font rotation.
- If unavailable, use a consistent close fallback (bold condensed sans for display; clean sans for body) and disclose the substitution.

### On-screen typography rules
- The opening hook is a short, concrete phrase that intensifies the first visual beat. Typography must not delay the story or replace the action.
- Use selective emphasis: one key word or short phrase at a time. Warm Ivory is default; Vermilion is rare.
- Subtitles are a separate layer from hook/emphasis text. Keep them accurate and readable over moving scenes.
- Never animate every spoken word. Use controlled reveals, tracking, position, or scale only when they strengthen the emotional beat.
- Keep text secondary to character expression and physical action unless typography itself is the story device.
- No forced text on every shot. Preserve clean frames when the image communicates better alone.
- Maintain clear hierarchy, spacing, alignment, and safe margins in both 16:9 and 9:16. Never cover faces, key props, or action.
- **Approved high-impact treatments:** (1) one short hook appearing with the opening action; (2) one isolated key word briefly switching to Muted Vermilion at the emotional turn; (3) a restrained title-card phrase in Oswald with generous negative space; (4) a minimal Manrope subtitle treatment that stays out of the character's face/action. Use one treatment at a time. No bouncing word-by-word karaoke captions, meme fonts, glowing outlines, or noisy kinetic presets.

### Brand signature
Use the canonical Ninety Figure and cube references when supplied. Preserve their exact silhouette, proportions, geometry, material, and established colors. The recurring “90” mark may appear as a restrained signature, not a watermark on every frame.

## Sound design, text, and pacing — mandatory

Load and follow `references/sound-design-workflow.md` for the complete sound map, music editing, ambience, foley, transitions, mixing, source discipline, and final audio QC. The direction below defines the Ninety Sense-specific sonic identity.

**Sound is a core retention tool for Ninety Sense. A voiceover-only soundtrack is not acceptable.** Build a detailed, story-driven soundscape that makes the animated world feel alive and makes every emotional beat land.

For each scene, deliberately layer:
- **Background music:** use emotionally specific music that evolves with the story—unease, curiosity, awkwardness, anxiety, suspense, realization, sadness, relief, or dark humor as appropriate. Build tension, drops, rises, and payoff around the narration. Avoid one flat generic music loop.
- **Environmental ambience:** add setting-specific atmosphere such as bedroom room tone, distant traffic, crowd murmur, office hum, rain against a window, hallway tone, or other sounds that make the 3D environment believable.
- **Story-specific SFX:** use varied, precisely timed sounds for visible actions and emotional cues: phone buzzes, notification pings, typing, message send sounds, footsteps, clothing movement, chair scrapes, doors, breathing, clock ticks, objects being placed or dropped, room movement, and other effects that the actual story calls for. Make the effects match the action; do not sprinkle random sounds.
- **Transitions and emotional accents:** subtle risers, impacts, reverse swells, whooshes, or brief stingers can mark a reveal or transition, but only when motivated. Avoid repetitive whooshes on every cut.
- **Voiceover:** remains the emotional center and must always be intelligible. Duck music and ambience under speech; use narration gaps for selected foreground effects.

Create a sound map alongside the visual shot plan. Mark the exact narration beat/action, SFX, ambience, music movement, and sync point. Sound effects should reinforce the physical action and psychology, not merely decorate it. Use intentional silence sparingly for a powerful pause, never as a substitute for completing the soundtrack.

Open on the most intriguing action or consequence. Use one short hook and selective emphasis; subtitles are separate. Do not animate every spoken word. Cut on changes in action, reaction, or idea; avoid meaningless rapid cuts. Sync music changes and effects to the emotional progression. The soundtrack should have purposeful variation across the entire video.

## Content integrity
No invented psychological research, fake quotations, diagnoses, or copied scripts. Every scene must advance the actual narration.


## Image prompt handoff

For every Ninety Sense production, follow the shared mandatory workflow in `references/image-prompt-workflow.md`. Claude must understand the actual script/story and produce detailed, ready-to-paste prompts for only the images the edit needs. Prompts must preserve Ninety Sense's stylized 3D environments and integrated flat 2D expressive-face identity, recurring-character continuity, and the selected 16:9 or 9:16 composition. The user generates the images in their chosen tool and uploads them labelled Image 1, Image 2, etc. Do not assume or require a connected image-generation API. Pause for required image uploads before claiming the video is complete.
