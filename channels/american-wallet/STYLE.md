# American Wallet — channel style profile

## Audience and promise
American personal-finance audience, especially older adults and people approaching or living in retirement. The video should make financial consequences understandable and emotionally relevant without talking down to viewers.

## Editorial identity
Cinematic financial documentary storytelling. Use clear explanatory storytelling and visual evidence. Vox-style visual explanation can inform maps, diagrams, data visualization, and visual clarity, but do not copy another publisher's branding, layouts, or signature graphics.

## Visual language
- Brand colors: Deep Navy `#14263D` and Warm Ivory `#F5F1E8`.
- Do not introduce gold or unrelated accent colors as brand colors.
- Use realistic, grounded environments and meaningful financial objects.
- Visual metaphors may include bills, grocery receipts, retirement statements, clocks, homes, savings jars, bank screens, and changing price tags.
- Use charts, maps, timelines, document close-ups, and animated comparisons when the story contains information that benefits from explanation.
- Keep financial visuals credible. Never invent balances, charts, government forms, official seals, or statistics.
- If a supplied host reference image is used, preserve the person's identity and recognizable appearance. Do not replace it with a generic younger presenter.

## Typography and emphasis
- Highlight important dollar amounts, dates, thresholds, and short phrases at the exact narration moment.
- Use short, high-contrast emphasis rather than paragraphs.
- Keep text readable for older viewers and on mobile.
- Subtitles and designed emphasis are separate systems.

## Sound
- Documentary atmosphere: restrained, thoughtful, tense when warranted, reassuring only when earned.
- Use subtle paper, room, calculator, keyboard, cash-register, household, or environmental sounds when they fit the scene.
- Music must not make a factual explanation feel more sensational than it is.

## Story rules
- Start with the viewer's real financial concern, not a generic introduction.
- Explain what changes, who is affected, when it matters, and what the viewer should understand.
- Use specific evidence and consequences.
- Politics may be context or a hook when relevant, but the channel remains finance-first.
- Never fabricate current events, policy details, dates, or financial advice.
- Do not add a subscribe prompt unless the user specifically requests one.

## Format adaptation
For 16:9, use documentary environments and layered explanatory compositions. For 9:16, simplify each frame to one primary idea, enlarge figures, and recompose charts and text rather than cropping.
