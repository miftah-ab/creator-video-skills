# Ninety Sense — production profile

## Core identity
Psychological and emotional Shorts for viewers interested in overthinking, relationships, identity, loneliness, behavior, and uncomfortable truths. The content should feel like a short animated story, not a motivational quote slideshow.

## Required visual direction
Use the mixed-media visual grammar documented in `../../skills/voiceover-to-video/references/ricks-clips-visual-system.md`:
- Stylized 3D environments, props, lighting, and character bodies.
- Distinctive flat 2D expressive face/head graphics integrated onto characters where appropriate.
- Clear physical action that makes an invisible psychological state visible.
- Strong character continuity across shots.
- Readable, uncluttered compositions and quick visual comprehension on mobile.

This is a visual-format reference, not permission to copy Ricks Clips' scripts, characters, exact designs, footage, voice, music, or branding. Build original Ninety Sense characters and scenes.

## Story adaptation
Do not force the reference channel's unusual-prop / cause-and-effect story formula onto every psychology script. Borrow the visual storytelling mechanics:
- Show a recognizable behavior or emotional consequence immediately.
- Let the viewer understand the situation before explaining it.
- Escalate through visible choices, reactions, and consequences.
- Reveal the psychological insight through an action or visual reversal.
- End on a memorable emotional payoff.

Avoid generic advice, random cinematic b-roll, floating quote text, and unrelated luxury imagery.

## Character design rules
- Establish a small reusable cast with consistent body proportions, outfit, face treatment, and color identity.
- Use expressive readable faces: confusion, suspicion, anxiety, embarrassment, anger, relief, realization.
- Keep the 2D face treatment visibly graphic and intentionally integrated with the 3D scene; do not drift into generic photorealistic human faces.
- Preserve identity, clothing, props, and spatial continuity between adjacent shots.
- Use new character designs; do not recreate identifiable Ricks Clips characters.

## Text and pacing
- The first visual must communicate the hook without requiring a paragraph.
- Use a short hook caption and selective emphasis only.
- Do not put every spoken word on screen as designed typography. Subtitles remain separate.
- Cut on changes in action, reaction, or idea. Avoid meaningless rapid cuts.
- Keep all essential text large and central enough for phone viewing.

## Sound
- Voiceover is the narrative center.
- Use music to support tension, awkwardness, suspense, or emotional release.
- Use effects for meaningful actions and reveals, not every cut.
- Silence is allowed and sometimes stronger than another impact sound.

## Non-negotiable
- No placeholder assets in a finished delivery.
- No invented psychological research, quotes, or diagnoses.
- No copied scripts, characters, music, or footage from the reference channel.
- Every scene must earn its place by advancing the story.
